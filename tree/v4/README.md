# Mother tree v4 (draft, 3 November 2026): levers in place of pathways

v3's bottom level was the literature's causal pathways, and plans do not speak that language: the Thai v3 run put 13% of strategies on a pathway. v4 keeps v3's groups and families and puts a lever under each family: what a government does that moves the family's purpose, in a plan's own grain. The pathways stay in the tree under the lever they belong to, as the evidence behind it, and as an optional depth.

Levels under the RHDR pillars and subpillars:

- `groups_v4.csv`: 33 groups (L1), unchanged from v3.
- `families_v4.csv`: 60 families (L2), unchanged from v3, with the ids of their levers.
- `levers_v4.csv`: 229 levers (L3), the placement level. Each carries its `joins_when` and `joins_not` tests (prose, judged by meaning), its pathways, the union of their text cues and risk tags, the Thai entry points of the bridge face, and the literature of its pathways in `evidence`. 198 levers rest on one pathway, 28 on two, 3 on three.
- `pathways_v4.csv`: 263 pathways (L4), v3's rows (266 less the 3 still merged into a sibling) with the `lever_id` they sit under. Columns as v3 plus the bridge face: lever, joins_when, joins_not.

Depth rule: every strategy that names an action is placed on a lever, judged by meaning with the lever's tests. A summary line with no action stays on its family. Where no lever of the family describes the action, the strategy stays on the family and `reason` says what lever is missing: a lever is not forced where none exists. A pathway is recorded only when the lever has one pathway or the strategy's wording names the channel; the text cues are an advisory flag, never a veto. A group alone is a weak placement and is counted.

`tree_v4.json` holds everything in one file; `meta.merged_pathways` lists the 3 merged pathways.

## How the levers were made

Each v3 pathway got a bridge face on 3 November (`../v3/bridge.py`): a lever phrase, a joins-when test, a joins-not test. v4 turns those into nodes. `LEVER_MERGES` in `build.py` lists the 31 places where several sibling pathways are one action for a government (for example contributory insurance, income replacement in shocks and unemployment insurance, which a plan writes as one social-insurance line); the lever then carries all its pathways and a joins-when test written for the merged lever. Everything else is one lever per pathway. Lever ids are LV-001 to LV-229 in family order; the short lever names are cut from the bridge phrase and are for the reviewers to rename.

## Intervention-level evidence on the levers (scan of 3 November 2026)

The pathways carry the mechanism literature (why a lever works). Each lever also carries one or two sources on the intervention itself: a systematic review or meta-analysis where one exists (3ie, Campbell, Cochrane, J-PAL, World Bank, IEG, OECD, ILO, IFPRI), otherwise a flagship evaluation or landmark empirical paper, with evidence from developing and middle-income countries preferred. Columns on `levers_v4.csv` and in `tree_v4.json`:

- `intervention_evidence`: "Author Year, Title, Outlet", one or two sources.
- `evidence_finding`: one sentence on what the evidence finds, with the main condition under which the lever works or fails.
- `evidence_strength`: strong (consistent evidence from reviews or several rigorous studies), mixed (effects depend on design or context), thin (few rigorous studies), none found.
- `evidence_verified`: yes when the source was confirmed online by title and author at the scan; `evidence_url` is where. A source that could not be confirmed is not kept: the lever reads "No suitable intervention-level source confirmed".

Second pass (3 November, later the same day): every source was read (abstract or full text) against its lever; the finding was rewritten from the text read, with `evidence_basis` saying what in the text supports it; `evidence_design` records the study design, `evidence_fit` how directly the source evaluates the lever's action (direct, partial, loose), `evidence_checked` how much was read. Sources that could not be read were dropped, and 48 were replaced with a better review or evaluation. Strength was re-graded on the sources read only, so one or two agreeing studies count as thin: 24 strong, 97 mixed, 106 thin, 2 none found (LV-096 long-term fiscal planning, LV-109 tourism market diversification). Entries the scan itself flags as weak for an economists' review, by fit or design: LV-005, 008, 016, 017, 021, 030, 041, 043, 047, 055, 058, 067, 080, 091, 095, 108, 113, 119, 123, 132, 137, 152, 153, 164, 171, 172, 173, 177, 183, 186, 190, 200, 209, 218, 219.

The scan was done by model workers with web search and page reads; it is a checked first pass for the economists' review, not a settled evidence base. Source: `inputs/lever_evidence.json`; the build reads it when present.

Framework anchoring (`inputs/framework_refs.json`, columns `framework_source`, `framework_where`, `framework_says`, `framework_refs`; read 3 November 2026 from the full texts of 19 documents): the framework document behind each group, read against each lever: what it says about the action, on which printed page, and the references it cites for it, checked against the document's own reference list (a reference the reader could not find there was left out). Documents by subpillar: TSE Inclusion Matters (2013); CAP Human Development Reports 1990 and 1994; FUT Changing Wealth of Nations 2021, IPCC AR6 WGII and WGIII (Summary for Policymakers and Technical Summary) and the ILO just-transition guidelines (2015); EXP the Growth Report (2008) and WDR 2020; DOM WDR 2009; GTI WDR 2008, Finance for All? (2008) and Inclusive Growth Analytics (2009); REF WDR 2017, WDR 2004, Fox 2015 and DLP 2018. Result: 189 of 229 levers anchored to a document, 151 of them discussed in it and 38 not (then `framework_says` says what the document leaves out); 241 references in all. Thesis literature (`inputs/thesis_refs.json`, columns `thesis_n`, `thesis_citations`, `thesis_refs`): papers from the economics literature library behind the thesis (207 catalogued, 160 held; firm dynamics, misallocation, entrepreneurship, mobility, microenterprise; identification tagged RCT, natural experiment, structural and so on) whose intervention or setting matches a lever, with the identification design and a one-line finding. The match found 24 levers with at least one paper (39 references: 26 RCT, 10 quasi-experimental, 3 structural); the library is about firms and workers, so most of the tree has no match, and that is expected. Loose fits kept and flagged: LV-011, LV-024, LV-041, LV-054, LV-164, LV-227. The file is written on the laptop and is being transferred; the build runs without it.

## Independent review (4 November 2026)

An independent reviewer reviewed an older table of 3 families and 18 levers, not tree v4, and supplied 27 references and 72 links from a reference to one of their levers (36 core, 36 complementary). `inputs/reviewer_crosswalk.json` maps their work onto v4: the three pillars are the same, and their 18 levers are close to our 10 subpillars (for example P1 is our Tackling Structural Exclusion, M4 our anticipation groups), so each of their levers is mapped onto the groups and families it covers, with secondary families where it reaches into another cut, and a note where the cut differs.

The references sit on the families as theory anchors (`theory_anchors`, `theory_anchor_ids`, `theory_n` on `families_v4.csv`). A family carries a reference with the link's role, core or complementary, when the reviewer lever behind the link maps onto the family, and as secondary when the family is only a secondary match; a reference linked several times keeps its strongest role and lists every reviewer lever it came through (`via`). The levers inherit their family's anchors (`theory_anchor_ids`, `theory_n` on `levers_v4.csv`).

Each lever also gets a backing grade (`framework_status`, `backing_score`, `backing_grade`), built by `build.py` from what the tree already holds. `framework_status` is "none" when the lever has no framework document (the governance levers), "not discussed" when `framework_says` says the document leaves the action out, "weak" for the 33 anchors flagged weak on 3 November (`WEAK_ANCHORS`), else "discussed". Points: framework discussed 2, weak 1, not discussed or none 0; evidence strength strong 3, mixed 2, thin 1, none found 0; evidence fit partial minus 0.5, loose minus 1, direct 0; at least one thesis paper plus 1. Grade: a score of 4.5 or more is well backed, 3 up to 4.5 backed, 1.5 up to 3 thin, below 1.5 unbacked. `thai_strategies` counts the rows of `thailand/strategies.csv` whose `lever_id` is the lever; it is shown beside the grade and does not count in it.

The workbook has a Codebook sheet, one row per lever in the structure the reviewer used: operational definition, framework anchor, what the framework says, causal mechanism and scope conditions (from the pathways), include and exclude tests, competing levers (the levers the joins-not test points to), plan-language examples, intervention evidence, thesis papers, theory anchors, framework references, Thai strategies, threats to validity (written from the flags above) and the backing grade. Reviewers still have to write the observable indicators; that column is empty. The Backing, Backing summary, Theory anchors and Reviewer crosswalk sheets show the grade with the gaps first, the counts by subpillar, the 27 references and the 18 reviewer levers.

## Literature-driven check of the levers (4 November 2026)

The levers and their tests were drafted by hand on 3 November and the literature was attached afterwards, so the joins were traceable but not validated. On 4 November every lever's name, joins_when and joins_not were read against the literature now attached to it (the pathways' causal chain, conditions and distinctions; the intervention evidence and its finding; the framework text; the thesis papers; the family's theory anchors). The result is `inputs/lever_check.json`, one record per lever with a verdict, what was checked (name, tests, literature fit), the literature item that justifies the verdict, and a note. `build.py` applies it: a "fix" replaces the name or a test and keeps the draft wording in `lever_draft`, `joins_when_draft`, `joins_not_draft`; a "flag" changes nothing and is left for the economists. Columns `check_verdict`, `check_name_ok`, `check_tests_ok`, `check_literature_ok`, `check_source`, `check_note` on `levers_v4.csv`; the Lever check sheet of the workbook lists flags and fixes first.

Result: 159 keep, 42 fix, 28 flag. Of the fixes, 7 rename the lever (cut-off or tool-only names: LV-007, 016, 021, 042, 046, 058, 064), 14 rewrite joins_when so the test names what the attached evidence evaluates (for example groundwater control through pump electricity, management practices in technology adoption, subsidised efficiency upgrades), and 32 rewrite joins_not. The commonest defect was in the 31 merged levers: their joins_not was built by joining the member pathways' exclusions, so it excluded the lever's own pathways (LV-020, 028, 065, 085, 090, 093, 105, 114, 115, 120, 130, 139, 157, 162, 164, 167 and others); the fixes point the exclusions at the siblings instead. The 28 flags are of three kinds: the attached evidence evaluates a sibling's action, not this lever's (LV-129, 145, 151, 154, 140); the lever bundles two actions the literature treats differently (LV-124, 133, 147, 169, 160, 163); or the literature contradicts the lever's premise (LV-037 drug enforcement and violence, LV-152 large-scale farming, LV-087 cleaner production without binding regulation, LV-128 e-commerce income for producers, LV-098). Flagged: LV-010 Family support, parenting and household programmes; LV-037 Enforcement against trafficking, drug markets and organised crime; LV-068 Transition support for workers, firms and places hit by decarbonisation; LV-078 Governance of forest rents; LV-081 Water pricing, allocation rules, conservation and efficiency measures; LV-087 Cleaner production, resource efficiency and green industry programmes; LV-097 Family planning, reproductive health and fertility policy; LV-098 Savings and financial institutions for a working-age population; LV-124 National road, rail and inland transport networks; LV-128 E-commerce platforms, digital trade skills and delivery; LV-129 Reliable electricity, gas and network supply for production; LV-133 Supply-chain linkages, local processing and local content; LV-140 Rural non-farm enterprise and job creation; LV-145 Financial sector development, capital markets, supervision; LV-147 Savings products and financial literacy; LV-151 Irrigation and on-farm water management; LV-152 Land consolidation, large-scale farming and mechanisation; LV-154 Collection points, rural market infrastructure and transport to market; LV-160 Reskilling and support for workers displaced by automation; LV-163 Legal migration pathways and recruitment regulation; LV-166 Commercial courts, contract enforcement, dispute settlement; LV-169 Land redistribution, agrarian reform and land taxation for equity; LV-173 Reducing concentration and dependence in trade and supply; LV-175 Strategic capacity and stocks of critical goods; LV-176 Disaster insurance, contingent credit and emergency financing frameworks; LV-191 Performance management, autonomy and incentives; LV-222 Community-driven development and community enterprise funds; LV-223 Support for civil society and volunteer networks.

## Double coding (4 November 2026)

A stratified random sample of 119 of the Thai 13th Plan's 519 strategies (by the plan's own pillar, seed 13) was coded blind by two independent coders from the codebook alone (family first, then the lever by its tests; strategy text and the codebook, nothing else), and compared with the run's placement (gpt-5.4, M_map_v4). Script `double_coding.py`; inputs in `inputs/double_coding/`; rows in `thailand/plan_13th/double_coding.csv`; the Double coding and Double coding summary sheets of the workbook.

Agreement (Cohen's kappa in brackets): coder 1 vs coder 2, family 91% (0.90), lever 84% (0.83); run vs coder 1, family 62% (0.61), lever 56% (0.54); run vs coder 2, family 59% (0.57), lever 52% (0.50). All three agree on the family for 68 strategies and on the lever for 58. The run put 104 of the 119 on a lever, the coders 99 and 95.

Reading: the codebook is codable, since two blind coders agree on the family nine times in ten and on the lever five times in six; the run is the outlier. In 40 strategies the two coders agree with each other and the run differs at family level, and in most of those the run's placement reads as a word match (a "pilot" programme for AI skills placed on pilots and sandboxes, LV-184, instead of digital skills, LV-062; smart-electronics incentives on LV-143 instead of infant-industry support, LV-112) or a drift into the governance subpillars (LV-196, 222, 190 are the run levers neither coder chose). Caveats: the two coders are model readers of the same kind, so their agreement overstates what two economists would reach; the run saw the extraction's context and the coders saw the strategy text only; and the sample is 119 rows. What follows from it: the run's placements of the 13th Plan should be re-done with the mapping prompt tightened against word matches (the coders' rule, family first then the lever's tests), which is a paid re-run; and the double coding is the test to repeat after the economists' review.

## Pathways: what they add

The pathways are not a placement level in practice. Of the 519 strategies, 398 carry a pathway id, but 370 of those are automatic (the lever has one pathway) and only 28 were placed because the strategy's wording named the channel. What the 263 pathway rows hold that the levers do not: the causal chain (input, transmission, outcome) and its conditions, 419 distinct mechanism citations (432 in all, 254 "cited, not yet independently verified"), and the distinctions between siblings from which the joins_not tests were written. All of that could sit on the lever as columns; the 31 levers with two or three pathways would carry two or three chains. The pathway id is still what joins earlier outputs (v1 to v3) to the tree.

## Rebuild

```bash
cd tree/v4 && python3 build.py
```

Reads `../v3/tree_v3.json` (which carries the bridge face) and the inputs, applies `inputs/lever_check.json`, and writes the four CSVs and the JSON. `python3 double_coding.py` rescores the double coding; `python3 tree_xlsx.py` writes the workbook; `python3 viewer.py` the viewer. The build fails if a pathway is left without a lever or a lever without a family.
