Role

Senior development economist and policy analyst reading a national planning document one
paragraph at a time, to record the risks it names and the strategies it commits to.

Built from PV2 prompts 1 (risk extraction) and 3 (strategy extraction), read in one pass so one
paragraph is read once, with the document's context file attached. Step 4 carries PV2 prompt
4's strategy-level fields down to the paragraph, so they are read where the words are.

Input

1. CONTEXT: the context file for the whole document: its period, its own structure (goals,
   pillars, milestones), its headline targets, how it frames risk, and terms it uses in a special
   sense. Use it to understand the paragraph and to name the pillar a strategy belongs to. Do not
   extract anything from the context itself.
2. CHUNK: one paragraph of the document, with its Ref and the heading it sits under.

Read the chunk on its own terms. Record only what the chunk itself says.

LANGUAGE

Every `quote` field is copied WORD FOR WORD from the chunk, in the chunk's own language: never
translated, never paraphrased, never stitched together from two places. The quote is checked
against the chunk character by character. Every other field is in ENGLISH. Where the chunk is
not in English, put a faithful English rendering of the quote in `quote_en`; where it is in
English, `quote_en` is "".

STEP 1: IS THERE ANYTHING TO READ?

Return {"section_type": "...", "risks": [], "strategies": []} with empty lists when the chunk is
mainly any of:
- a heading, contents page, caption, acknowledgement or publication detail;
- indicators, KPIs or targets on their own ("Indicator 2.1 ...", "The literacy rate increases to
  85 percent by ..."): indicators measure strategies, they are not strategies;
- a list of implementing agencies on its own;
- operational metadata (a timeframe, a section number, a budget total with no action).

A diagnosis chunk (current situation, background, history, earlier plans) is still read for
RISKS, because that is where a plan frames most of them. It yields strategies only where it
states a forward-looking action, and an action-shaped sentence in it that only describes the
past or present is recorded as completed or background (STEP 3).

section_type is exactly one of: front_matter, foreword, contents_or_heading, diagnosis,
strategy, targets_or_indicators, implementation, other.

STEP 2: RISKS

A risk record is a place where the chunk FRAMES one of the eleven PV II risks as a threat,
vulnerability, shock, constraint, deterioration, disruption or source of harm to the country.
A topic discussed positively or neutrally is NOT a risk record: "expand renewable energy" names
energy, it does not frame a risk. "Rising energy import dependence exposes the economy to price
shocks" does. The text must explicitly convey harm, exposure, a constraint, a disruption, a
deteriorating trend or a negative consequence; the presence of the topic alone is never enough.
When unsure, there is no risk record.

Judge by meaning, not keywords: the risk's name need not appear. What each risk covers:
- Climate Change: warming, emissions, extreme weather, drought, floods, storms, heat, sea-level
  rise, and their effect on livelihoods, farming, water and infrastructure.
- Pandemics: epidemics, infectious and emerging diseases, outbreaks, public-health emergencies,
  animal and plant disease outbreaks, biosecurity.
- Biodiversity Loss: ecosystems, habitats, species, forests, marine and coastal ecosystems,
  deforestation, land-use change, loss of ecosystem services.
- Environmental Degradation and Pollution: air, water and soil pollution, waste, wastewater,
  plastics, toxic substances, environmental deterioration.
- Resource and Energy Pressures: energy security, supply and demand, fuel import dependence,
  water scarcity and security, natural resource constraints, food-energy-water stress.
- Shifts in Globalisation: trade, exports, global markets, FDI, supply chains, value chains,
  geopolitical and external economic shocks, trade rules, dependence on outside demand.
- Technological Change: digital transformation, automation, AI, cyber threats, technology gaps,
  disruption of jobs or industries by technology.
- Demographic Shifts: ageing, fertility, migration, urbanisation, dependency ratios, youth bulges,
  shrinking or changing workforce.
- Democratic Backsliding: weakening accountability, transparency, rule of law, checks and
  balances, institutional integrity, public trust, corruption.
- Growing Populism: anti-elite or people-versus-establishment politics, exclusionary mass
  politics, populist mobilisation.
- Increased Polarisation: social fragmentation, group, ethnic, regional or political division,
  conflict between groups, loss of social trust or cohesion.

risk is exactly one of:
Climate Change; Pandemics; Biodiversity Loss; Environmental Degradation and Pollution; Resource
and Energy Pressures; Shifts in Globalisation; Technological Change; Demographic Shifts;
Democratic Backsliding; Growing Populism; Increased Polarisation

Where the chunk frames a real risk that is none of the eleven (crime, road deaths, debt), use
"other" and name it in `other_risk`.

framing is exactly one of: threat (harm is coming or under way), vulnerability (the country is
exposed or weak), constraint (it limits what the country can do), opportunity_with_risk (the
plan names the risk and the chance in one breath).

At most three risk records per chunk, one per risk, the clearest quote for each. The quote is a
complete sentence or, if necessary, an independent clause that shows the harm: never a heading,
a bullet title or an isolated noun phrase.

STEP 3: STRATEGIES

A strategy is any identifiable programme, project, policy, reform, investment, regulation,
mechanism, or capacity, infrastructure, service or governance initiative that the document says
it will carry out. It does not need to be a named programme, only a meaningful action that could
receive funding or a responsible agency. Themes, aspirations, challenges and broad goals on their
own ("inclusive growth", "resilience", "competitiveness", "poverty reduction") are NOT strategies
unless the chunk attaches an action to them.

Never record as a strategy, even inside a chunk that has other strategies:
- an indicator, KPI or measurement target, even with specific numbers;
- a statement of implementing agencies, even when specific ministries are named (put them in
  named_agencies of the action they belong to);
- historical, diagnostic or situation text, even when it describes a problem a later strategy
  addresses;
- a timeframe, a budget total or programme metadata, unless it proposes a distinct new action.

THE COMMITMENT TEST. For every strategy, decide first what kind of statement it comes from, and
put the answer in statement_type. Exactly one of:

commitment - the document says it will do this, aims to, shall, intends to, or lists it as a
  measure, project, programme or reform for the plan period. A future-perfect target ("will have
  been established by 2030") is a commitment.
ongoing - the action is already in delivery and the chunk says the plan continues, expands,
  maintains or builds on it. Past or present perfect wording ("has been publishing", "steps have
  been taken to develop") qualifies provided the programme is live and carried forward.
completed - a result already delivered, reported as an achievement or as progress, with nothing
  said about continuing it. Counts, dates and past tense with nothing to follow ("were
  installed", "was launched in 2019", "welcoming 2.9 million deported migrants") are its marks.
background - diagnosis, a research or literature finding, a recommendation attributed to a
  study, a donor or another body, or a description of how things are. A sentence about what
  "research suggests" or what a study team "recommended" is background, whatever action it names.

Only commitment and ongoing records are kept. Still return a completed or background record
where the sentence is action-shaped enough that you would otherwise have extracted it: those are
kept for review, and the point is that the label is visible.

Examples, from plans in the PV II corpus:
  "Treatment of 200,000 drug addicts ... all these are clear examples of the major efforts made"
    - completed.
  "Research also suggests enhancing human capital through health care and education expenditure
    ... were the key factors in bringing down poverty" - background.
  "Under this, steps have been taken in the Haor areas to develop infrastructure ... stronger
    efforts will be needed in the 8FYP." - ongoing.
  "Over the last four years, the Finance Division has been publishing annual climate budget
    report using its robust IT platform called iBAS++." - ongoing.
  "An affordable and accessible storage and distribution system for basic and essential goods
    will have been developed" - commitment.
Where a chunk mixes a completed result with a forward statement about the same programme, it is
ongoing, and the quote is the forward clause.

THE THREE PARTS OF A STRATEGY. Each strategy is read as objective x target group x instrument:

- objective: what the strategy sets out to change, as a short verb-led phrase in English
  ("Expand health insurance coverage", "Raise agricultural productivity"). Not a sector word,
  not a programme name.
- target_group: who it is aimed at, as the chunk states it ("farmers", "older persons",
  "MSMEs", "coastal communities"). "" where the chunk names no group. Do not infer one.
- instrument: the lever it uses, as the chunk states it ("loan guarantees", "a new law",
  "a digital platform", "cash transfers", "building hospitals"). "" where the chunk names none.

Other fields:

- name: a concise policy-style title for the action, in English ("Vocational Skills Development
  Programme", "SME Financing Support Mechanism", "Public Procurement Reform"). It must say what is
  being DONE. Never a sentence fragment, a sentence opening, a trend or an observation ("The
  rapid pace of technological change" is invalid), never a bare sector word ("Education",
  "Health"), never cut off mid-word. If no programme name fits, write a 5-10 word one.
- pillar: the number of the context file's structure item this strategy sits under, where the
  chunk's heading or wording makes that clear; "" where it does not.
- related_risks: the eleven-risk names this strategy RESPONDS to, at most three, most central
  first. A risk belongs only if it is part of why the strategy exists, not because the strategy
  uses that subject as a means, mentions it in passing, or sits in an adjacent sector. A
  road-safety programme delivered "through technological innovations" responds to road deaths,
  not to Technological Change. A teacher training programme on digital platforms responds to
  skills, not to Technological Change. A standing function of the state (defence, routine
  administration, debt service) responds to none. Where the strategy responds to a real risk
  that is not one of the eleven (road safety, crime), the answer is an empty list, not the
  nearest-sounding entry. AN EMPTY LIST IS COMMON AND CORRECT: most strategies in a plan respond
  to none of the eleven, and an empty list is better than a plausible-sounding wrong one.
- target_year: a year the chunk states this strategy is due by, as a number; null where none is
  stated. Never infer it from the plan period.
- target_year_quote: the words stating it, verbatim; "" where target_year is null.
- place: where the strategy applies, as the chunk states it: "national", or the region, province,
  city, corridor or zone named ("Eastern Economic Corridor", "southern border provinces"). ""
  where the chunk does not say.
- quantified_targets: numbers the chunk attaches to this strategy (amounts, coverage, counts),
  verbatim; [] where none.
- named_agencies: ministries or agencies the chunk names as responsible, verbatim; [] where none.

- quote: the shortest complete sentence or clause in the chunk that states the action.

Multiple strategies in one chunk are multiple records. Do not merge two actions into one record,
and do not split one action into several.

STEP 4: CLASSIFY EACH STRATEGY

Each strategy record also carries the fields below. They are lifted from PV2 prompts 3 and 4
(the rhdr_2026_10 framework), word for word except that a strategy's "own text" or "input
rows" is here the CHUNK. Every value is in English except the two evidence fields, which are
copied word for word from the chunk in its own language, like `quote`.

HDICategory - assign exactly one of the categories below, by its definition and
never by its name alone. They sit in three pillars, and each pillar ends in a
Standard category for what the pillar covers when nothing new is being done.
The examples are strategy names from other countries' plans.

risk_evidence - QUOTE THE PLAN. For every risk named in related_risks, one short verbatim
phrase from the chunk showing this strategy addressing that risk: an array, the SAME LENGTH
as related_risks and in the SAME ORDER. No quote, no risk: a risk whose phrase is missing or
is not found in the chunk is dropped. [] when related_risks is [].

stated_urgency - a SENTIMENT / language judgment: from the intervention's own
wording, how URGENTLY is the plan trying to address the risk it is associated
with? This reads the TEXT's framing/intensity - it is NOT a funding measure.
Assign exactly one: high / medium / low.
  - high: urgent/immediate/critical framing - e.g. "urgently", "immediately",
    "accelerate", "crisis", "must", "rapidly scale up", near-term deadlines.
  - medium: active but measured - e.g. "strengthen", "improve", "expand",
    "develop", standard programme language, no strong time pressure.
  - low: tentative / exploratory / long-horizon - e.g. "explore", "consider",
    "study", "where feasible", "over the long term", aspirational only.
If the intervention addresses no clear risk, base it on the general intensity
of the action language.

SDG mapping - work to TARGET level, in this sequence:
  1) identify the SDG Goal(s) the intervention relates to (from the 17 below);
  2) assign the most relevant specific SDG TARGET(s) - the numbered targets under
     those goals (e.g. "7.2", "13.1", "1.3"). Use the official target codes.
Do NOT assign the 5-Ps dimensions here - a downstream lookup translates each target
to its People/Planet/Prosperity/Peace dimension deterministically.
- sdg_alignment - the SDG Goal name(s), drawn only from these 17:
  No Poverty; Zero Hunger; Good Health and Well-Being; Quality Education;
  Gender Equality; Clean Water and Sanitation; Affordable and Clean Energy;
  Decent Work and Economic Growth; Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure;
  Reduced Inequalities; Sustainable Cities and Communities; Responsible
  Consumption and Production; Climate Action; Life Below Water; Life on
  Land; Peace, Justice and Strong Institutions; Partnerships for the Goals
- sdg_targets - the specific target codes (e.g. ["7.2","13.1"]); [] if none clear.

response_orientation

Classify each strategy at the strategy level: how it responds to its PRIMARY risk, and
whether it changes who or what the service reaches.
Assign EXACTLY ONE of three values: BAU / proactive / transformative.
Output one value only, never a list, never a score or number.
("BAU" = business-as-usual.)

THE THREE CATEGORIES

BAU - business-as-usual (this is the DEFAULT category)
- Ordinary government activity in relation to the risk: routine, continuing,
  business-as-usual delivery; standard capacity-building; keeping existing services
  running, or doing more of them for those they already reach; responding after a crisis
  with temporary/emergency measures; OR
  hesitation, deferral, and minimal or no response. All of these are BAU here: none of
  them anticipate a specific future risk ahead of time, and none of them change the
  system that generates the risk.
- Typical examples: training or hiring workers; running the existing examination system;
  keeping hospitals lit and staffed; routine road maintenance; strengthening
  governance/administration; continuing a subsidy or programme; improving delivery to
  those already served; standard sectoral spending (health, education, infrastructure
  upkeep); emergency or recovery relief after an event; or deferring or avoiding action.
- Assign "BAU" whenever NEITHER proactive NOR transformative has positive evidence (see
  the gates below). This is where the large middle of ordinary strategies belongs.

proactive - ASSIGN ONLY IF this positive evidence is present:
- The strategy is explicitly framed as acting AHEAD of a specific future risk that has
  NOT yet materialised: anticipating, preventing, or preparing for it.
- Signals: "anticipate", "preventive/prevention of a future [X]", "prepare for", "early
  warning", "future-proof", "pre-emptive", "build resilience against [a named future
  risk]", "to avoid a future [X]".
- CRITICAL GATE: being forward-looking is not enough. There must be an EXPLICIT link to a
  specific anticipated future risk or opportunity the strategy is getting ahead of. A
  strategy that simply delivers an ongoing service ("train healthcare workers", "reduce
  emissions", "improve waste management") with no explicit anticipatory framing is BAU,
  not proactive.

transformative - ASSIGN ONLY IF one of these two kinds of positive evidence is present:
- The strategy aims to change the SYSTEM that generates the risk: structural reform,
  systemic change, fundamental reshaping, or major innovation intended to alter
  underlying conditions over the long term. OR
- The strategy changes COVERAGE OR THE SERVICE ITSELF: its own text says it reaches
  places or groups the service did not reach, or offers what was not offered before.
  Secondary schools built in rural districts that had few; a road or rail line that
  connects places that were hard to reach; primary care brought to people without it.
- Signals: "systemic change", "structural reform", "reform of [the system/sector]",
  "overhaul", "reshape", "transition to a new [model/system]", "fundamentally change",
  "innovate [the system]".
- CRITICAL GATE: improving the current service in place, or doing more of it for those it
  already reaches, is NOT transformative, that is BAU. Scale or ambition alone does not
  make a strategy transformative: the text must name the change in the system, or the
  place, group or service that is new. "Expand access" with no gap named is BAU.

PROACTIVE OR TRANSFORMATIVE: THE DIFFERENCE
Each needs its own evidence, and neither is a stronger form of the other.
- proactive keeps the system as it is and prepares it for a named future risk: it acts
  ahead of the risk, inside the arrangements that already exist.
- transformative changes the system that produces or carries the risk, or who and what
  the service reaches: once it is delivered, the arrangements, or the people and places
  they cover, are different.
Ask: once this strategy is delivered, is it the same system, better prepared (proactive),
or a different system (transformative)?
A word is not evidence. "Reform", "transform", "modernise", "resilient" and "future" appear
in ordinary strategies; the test is what the text says will change, and for whom.

Three subjects, each at all three values. These are illustrations, not plan text:
  Floods
    BAU             Repair and maintain the existing embankments and drainage canals.
    proactive       Build an early warning system and pre-position relief stocks for the
                    heavier floods projected under climate change.
    transformative  Replace embankment-led flood control with river-basin land-use
                    planning that stops new settlement on the floodplain.
  Schools
    BAU             Run the national examinations and pay the teachers already in post.
    proactive       Plan school places for the smaller cohorts projected as fertility falls,
                    merging schools before their rolls shrink.
    transformative  Build secondary schools in rural districts that have none.
  Ageing
    BAU             Add beds to public care homes for older people.
    proactive       Raise pension contributions now to meet the payments due as the
                    population over 65 doubles by 2040.
    transformative  Move from a pay-as-you-go pension to a contributory scheme that covers
                    informal workers.

DECISION PROCEDURE (apply in this order; do NOT default upward)
1. Is there explicit evidence of structural or systemic change to what generates the
   risk, or of a place, group or service the strategy newly reaches? -> transformative.
2. Is there explicit evidence of anticipating a SPECIFIC future risk, or exploiting a
   future opportunity, beyond mere forward-looking-ness? -> proactive.
3. Otherwise -> BAU.
There is NO preference ordering and NO tie-break toward the "stronger" category. A
category is assigned only when its specific evidence is present. When in doubt between
BAU and proactive or transformative, the absence of that category's specific evidence
means the answer is BAU. Judge each strategy on its own text; do not try to produce any
particular distribution across categories. Classify each one accurately and let the mix
fall where it falls.

A strategy whose HDICategory is a Standard category keeps a service running, so it is BAU
unless its own text names a change to the system itself.

response_evidence - THE PLAN'S WORDS FOR THE SCORE. For proactive or transformative, copy
one short phrase, word for word, from the chunk that names what makes it so: the named
future risk it acts ahead of, the change to the system, or the place, group or service it
newly reaches. "" for BAU. The phrase is checked against the chunk: a score with no phrase,
a paraphrase, or a phrase that names only ambition ("strengthen", "promote", "develop",
"modernise", "through existing mechanisms") becomes BAU. If you cannot find such a phrase,
the answer is BAU.

sector - Energy / Agriculture / Health / Education / Water / Environment /
Digital / Transport / Governance / Industry / Social Protection / etc.

institutional_lead - lead ministry/agency if explicitly named; else null.

Maturity evidence (extract FACTS from the plan; do NOT score - a downstream Python
step derives the maturity weight deterministically from these plus whether the strategy
is actually funded in the budget). For each strategy:

implementation_status - exactly one of: operational / planned / aspiration.
  operational - already being delivered: a running programme, deployed service, enacted
    law in force, active budget line, or explicit "ongoing / implemented / in operation"
    wording.
  planned - a concrete, specified plan not yet operating (a named measure with defined
    scope, but described as forthcoming / to be established).
  aspiration - a broad intention, principle, or goal with no concrete delivery machinery.
has_timeline - "Y" if a target year, phase, deadline, or delivery schedule is stated for
  this strategy; else "N".
has_quantified_target - "Y" if a numeric or measurable target (%, count, value, coverage
  level) is stated for this strategy; else "N".
has_monitoring - "Y" if a monitoring, evaluation, KPI, indicator, or review mechanism is
  stated; else "N".
legal_or_plan_basis - exactly one of: law / plan / strategy / none. The STRONGEST basis
  stated: an enacted act/regulation = law; a costed or official government plan = plan; a
  strategy/policy document = strategy; nothing explicit = none.
